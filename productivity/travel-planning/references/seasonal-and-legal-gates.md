# Seasonal and legal gates

Some objectives are not "hard to book" — they are shut, and sometimes shut by law.
Resolve this before spending any effort on flights, hotels, or route timing.

## Gate types

| Gate | Typical form | Where it is published |
|---|---|---|
| Seasonal summit closure | Trails above a station closed from a date | Operator site, prefecture, park authority |
| Statutory closure | Entry prohibited with a penalty | Road Act / park ordinance text, prefectural page |
| Facility closure | Huts, toilets, rescue, water off | Facility operating-period table |
| Access-road closure | Toll road / pass gated for winter | Road operator status page |
| Permit or quota | Daily cap, lottery, timed entry | Booking system + official FAQ |
| Entry eligibility | Visa-free list, permit required, nationality-specific ban | Immigration authority / consulate |

## Verification recipe

1. Go to the **operator's own site** (`.go.jp`, `.jp` municipal, park authority). Guide blogs and OTA "best time to visit" pages are not evidence.
2. Find the current year's facility table (Japanese pages call it 施設の供用期間 or 営業期間). It lists each section separately — a summit can close while the 5th→6th station section stays open into November.
3. Find the closure notice (閉山 / 通行止め / 冬期閉鎖). Distinguish advisory from statutory: look for 道路法第46条 or a stated penalty.
4. Check the access road separately — it often closes later or earlier than the trail.
5. Record the exact date-time of closure, not just the date.

## Decision matrix when the objective is closed

| User's real goal | Offer |
|---|---|
| "A hard mountain weekend on these dates" | Same dates, nearby objective of comparable gain/time; give stats, turnaround time, and a bailout point |
| "THIS summit specifically" | Same objective, dates moved into the legal window; give booking lead time and the exact opening date |
| "We'll do it anyway" | State the legal penalty, absent rescue/huts, and recorded fatalities once; do not design the route |

Never silently substitute a different mountain under the original title — say what changed and why.

## Worked example — Mount Fuji

- **Season**: Yoshida and Subashiri Jul 1–Sep 10; Gotemba and Fujinomiya Jul 10–Sep 10. Fujinomiya's 5th→6th station section stays open into early November — still not a summit route.
- **Off-season status**: all summit trails closed under Road Act Art. 46; violation can bring up to 6 months' detention or a ¥300,000 fine. Huts, first-aid posts, and toilets closed; no trail maintenance, signage removed, weak cell coverage.
- **Conditions**: Nov–Apr summit minimum temperatures run below −10 °C; mean wind ≥15 m/s with gusts over 40 m/s. Official text notes experienced, well-equipped climbers have died.
- **Substitute used**: the Misaka range north of the lake — 十二ヶ岳 1,683 m → 節刀ヶ岳 1,736 m, ~10–11 km, +1,100–1,200 m, 6.5–7.5 h, T3 — full frontal Fuji view without entering the closed zone. Lighter: 三ツ峠山 (8 km / +750 m / 4.5 h). Harder: 天子山地 毛無山→竜ヶ岳 (16 km / +1,500 m / 9–10 h).

## Pitfalls

- **Entry eligibility is the first gate and it is passport-specific.** A route can be open, affordable, and perfectly timed while the traveller cannot legally board the flight — confirm nationality and visa basis before any route research. The same destination can be closed to one passport and open to another (mainland residents vs US passports for Taiwan).
- A guide company's "off-season climbing is permitted" page describes tolerated risk, not legality. Cite the road authority, not the vendor.
- Closure dates drift a few days each year — read the current year's table, never last year's blog post.
- A still-open lower section is the most common false green light; check the section that contains the summit.
